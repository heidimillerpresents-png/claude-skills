# Decision-Maker Outreach Drafter

## Purpose

A generic connection request or "I'd love to chat" message gets ignored. A message that shows real, specific knowledge of the person and why their situation matters right now gets a reply. This skill pairs quick research on a named decision-maker with a short, honest outreach draft, so every message sent is specific to that person rather than a template with their name swapped in.

This is a companion to the job-listing-strategic-analysis skill, not a replacement for it; if a strategic analysis of the company/role has already been run, pull directly from its findings (real priorities, pain points, organizational timing) rather than re-researching the company from scratch. If it hasn't been run and the situation calls for it, offer to run it first.

## Inputs needed

1. **The named person** — full name and title/role at the company. If the user hasn't named anyone, ask who the outreach is meant for; don't invent a generic recipient.
2. **The context** — is this a first-touch cold message, a thank-you after an interview, a follow-up after a referral, or a response to something the person posted/said publicly?
3. **The channel** — LinkedIn message, LinkedIn connection note (character-limited, keep it much shorter), or email.

## Process

1. **Research the person, not just the company.** Look for:
   - Their current role and how long they've held it
   - Anything they've posted, written, or said publicly (LinkedIn posts, company blog, interviews, panels) that reveals what they actually care about right now
   - Their likely priorities given their role and the company's current situation (pull from a prior strategic analysis if one exists)

2. **Find one honest, specific angle, not a list of flattering observations.** The best angle is usually one of:
   - A real, specific point of connection (shared prior colleague, a nomination came from them, they personally introduced themselves in a job posting)
   - A genuine observation about their public work that the user actually noticed and has a real reaction to
   - A direct, undisguised statement of why the user is reaching out (e.g. responding to a referral, following up after an interview)

   Avoid manufacturing false personal connection; if there's no genuine angle beyond "I saw the job posting," say so plainly and draft a message that owns that directness rather than performing false familiarity.

3. **Draft short.** LinkedIn messages and connection notes get worse the longer they get. Aim for a message the recipient can read in under 15 seconds; cut throat-clearing ("I hope this message finds you well") and get to the specific point fast.

4. **Match tone to context, not to a single formula:** a thank-you after a good interview should sound warm and appreciative; a cold first-touch should sound direct and low-pressure, with no ask beyond "I'd welcome the chance to talk"; a referral follow-up should name the referral by name early.

5. **Never fabricate specifics.** Don't invent an interaction, a shared connection, or a detail about the person's public work that wasn't actually verified in research; if research turns up nothing usable, say so, and default to a shorter, more direct message rather than padding it with invented color.

## Output

- The drafted message, ready to send, matched to the channel's length constraints
- A one-line note on the angle chosen and why, so the user can sanity-check it before sending
- If research turned up nothing specific and usable, say that directly rather than presenting a generic message as if it were personalized
