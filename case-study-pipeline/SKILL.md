# Case Study: Nomination to Draft Pipeline

## Purpose

Case studies fail when writers face a blank page with only scattered inputs (a nomination form, some call notes, a pile of interview transcripts) and no structure for turning them into a narrative. This skill breaks the process into five stages, each with a clear input, a clear output, and a validation checkpoint before moving on. The result: faster first drafts, consistent quality, and less rework at the review stage.

## Inputs needed

Before starting, confirm you have (or can get):
1. **Nomination submission form** — business context and eligibility details for why this customer was nominated
2. **Production readiness review** (or equivalent brief) — the customer's use case, results, and messaging details
3. **Kickoff call transcripts** — the account team's discussion with the customer, plus any follow-up calls
4. **The company's style guide and industry/audience messaging reference** — needed to ground the story in the company's actual voice and positioning rather than a generic template

If any of these are missing, ask what's available before starting Stage 1 — don't invent business context that hasn't been provided.

## Stage 1: Intake

Gather and read all raw material listed above before drafting anything. This stage produces no output on its own; it's the foundation for Stage 2.

## Stage 2: Alignment draft

Build a skeleton story that aligns the customer's specific story with the company's key messaging and industry pillars, in this order:

1. **Industry insight** (20-30 words): based only on the industry messaging reference and the nomination form, write a general industry insight — current trends and opportunities in this space. No mention of the customer yet.
2. **Audience**: who the story is written for (a specific role/title/persona, not a generic "business leader").
3. **Tension**: the specific pain this audience faces, without generic filler; it should connect clearly to what the breakthrough resolves.
4. **Breakthrough**: how the company's product/solution resolves that tension, stated as a genuine insight, not a feature list.
5. **Story summary** (120-150 words): using specific customer details from the production readiness review, write a summary that connects back to the audience, tension, and breakthrough above, and shows how this specific company exemplifies them. Return only the summary at this step, nothing else.

**Validation checkpoint before moving to Stage 3:** review the alignment draft against these checks, and flag (don't just silently fix) anything that fails:
- Does any section outside the story summary reference the specific customer? (It shouldn't — those sections should stay general/industry-level.)
- Does the tension clearly connect to the breakthrough?
- Is the audience specific and consistent throughout?
- Does the flow make sense: industry → audience → tension → insight → breakthrough?
- Is any section generic, vague, or repetitive?
- Does it align to the company's go-to-market messaging, not just the customer's own story?

## Stage 3: Interviews

Draft roughly 10 interview questions per interviewee, tailored to who they are:
- **End user** — day-to-day impact and workflow change
- **Technical implementer** — which products were used, why, how the rollout occurred, and results from a technical perspective
- **C-suite sponsor** — business challenges, outcomes, and ROI

Ground every question set in the approved alignment draft plus the Stage 1 materials, and make sure the specific products or features that must be highlighted (per the nomination form) are explicitly built into the questions. The account/project team schedules interviews; the writer conducts them.

## Stage 4: Drafting

Using the interview transcripts, the approved alignment draft, the production readiness review, and the brand style guide, draft the full case study (typically ~600 words unless the company's format differs). Guidelines:
- Open with the industry insight, and introduce the customer by the second sentence
- Use correct product names and formatting per the style guide throughout
- Every claim and quote should trace back to something actually said in the interview transcripts — never invent a quote or a result

**Validation checkpoint before this goes to the director/customer for approval:**
- Is the company's voice reflected throughout?
- Are quotes as strong as possible, and do they move the story forward (not just repeat a fact already stated in prose)?
- Would the target audience reading this say "I have that problem, and I know what it feels like"?
- Does it read as a specific, human story, not something that could apply to any company?
- Are all product names correct?

## Stage 5: Refine, approve, publish

Final human editorial pass:
- Strengthen and verify every quote against the transcript
- Sharpen any vague sections into specific pain points and claims
- Restore the human narrative where the draft has flattened into generic language
- Submit for stakeholder review

A good AI-assisted draft gets the case study to roughly 80% finished. The last 20% — narrative judgment, quote selection, making sure it reads as a real story — stays with the writer and shouldn't be shortcut.

## Notes on reuse

If prompts or checkpoints in this process turn out to work better in a different phrasing for a specific client or industry, that's expected — treat the stage structure as fixed and the exact prompt wording as adaptable per engagement.
